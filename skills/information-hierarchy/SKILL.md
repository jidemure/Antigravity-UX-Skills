# Information Hierarchy

Name one primary action, then secondary and tertiary actions. Avoid presenting competing actions as equal. Order information by task relevance and decision sequence; group related items by meaning, using spacing and headings before adding borders. Match density to frequency and consequence: frequent work benefits from compact scanning, while unfamiliar or high-risk tasks need explanation and breathing room. Check that labels say what will happen, not merely generic verbs. For tables, dashboards, and long forms, identify the key column/summary and preserve a clear reading path at smaller widths.
